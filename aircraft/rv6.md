## What Is It?

The Van’s RV-6 is a two-seat, low-wing airplane commonly built by individual owners from a kit. The pilot and passenger sit beside each other.

The original RV-6 uses conventional landing gear with a tailwheel. The closely related RV-6A uses tricycle landing gear, meaning it has two main wheels and a nose wheel.

The word **experimental** refers to its certification category. It does not mean the airplane is an unfinished prototype. Most RV-6 aircraft are registered as Experimental Amateur-Built aircraft because individuals completed most of the construction.

## What Was It Designed to Do?

The RV-6 was designed as an all-around sport airplane. It can be used for:

- Personal and recreational flying
- Cross-country travel
- Sightseeing
- Basic sport aerobatics
- Building and learning about aircraft systems

Van’s introduced the RV-6/6A in 1986. It became one of the most successful kit-aircraft designs ever produced. The RV-7/7A replaced it in Van’s product line in 2001.

## Major Features

- Two seats arranged side by side
- Wings positioned below the fuselage
- Traditional aluminum construction
- Fixed landing gear
- Bubble-style canopy with wide visibility
- Control sticks rather than control yokes
- Baggage compartment behind the seats
- RV-6 tailwheel and RV-6A nose-wheel configurations
- Builder-selected instruments, avionics, engine, and propeller

## How It Flies

The RV-6 is known for being fast, light, and responsive. Small movements of the control stick can produce noticeable changes in the airplane’s attitude.

Its low wing loading and broad speed range allow it to fly efficiently while remaining maneuverable. Van’s describes the RV family’s stall behavior as straightforward and predictable, although the airplane may provide relatively little aerodynamic warning before the stall occurs.

The RV-6 can perform sport aerobatics when the aircraft is properly configured, loaded, inspected, and operated by a qualified pilot. Aerobatic capability does not mean every maneuver is safe in every RV-6. The limitations assigned to the individual aircraft always control how it may be flown.

## Systems Worth Knowing

### Airframe

The RV-6 uses a primarily aluminum structure. Its wings are mounted below the fuselage, and fuel is carried in tanks built into the wings’ leading edges.

### Flight Controls

The pilot controls pitch and roll with a control stick. Pushrods connect the stick to the ailerons and elevator. Rudder pedals operate the rudder through cables.

### Engine

RV-6 aircraft commonly use four-cylinder Lycoming O-320 or O-360 engines producing approximately 150 to 180 horsepower.

Because each aircraft is completed by an individual builder, the exact engine installation may differ.

### Propeller

An RV-6 may have either:

- A **fixed-pitch propeller**, whose blade angle does not change in flight
- A **constant-speed propeller**, which automatically adjusts its blade angle to maintain a selected propeller speed

### Fuel System

The aircraft carries fuel in its wings. A cockpit selector allows the pilot to choose which tank supplies fuel to the engine.

### Landing Gear

The RV-6 has two main wheels and a tailwheel. The RV-6A replaces the tailwheel with a nose wheel, which generally makes ground handling more familiar to pilots trained in common tricycle-gear airplanes.

### Avionics

There is no single standard RV-6 instrument panel. Builders choose their own radios, navigation equipment, flight instruments, engine monitors, and electrical systems.

Two RV-6 aircraft may therefore have very different cockpits.

## Why It Matters

The RV-6 demonstrates that an airplane does not have to come from a conventional factory production line to become an important aviation design.

It also introduces several useful aviation concepts:

- Experimental amateur-built certification
- Aircraft kits and individual builders
- Tailwheel versus tricycle landing gear
- Fixed-pitch versus constant-speed propellers
- The relationship between aircraft weight, power, speed, and maneuverability
- Why pilots must understand the configuration of the specific aircraft they are flying

## Beginner Questions

### What does “RV” mean?

RV is the model prefix used by Van’s Aircraft, founded by aircraft designer Richard VanGrunsven.

### What is the difference between the RV-6 and RV-6A?

The RV-6 has a tailwheel. The RV-6A has a nose wheel and tricycle landing gear.

### Does “experimental” mean the aircraft is still being tested?

Not necessarily. In this case, it usually means the aircraft was built by an amateur builder and certified in the Experimental Amateur-Built category.

### Are all RV-6 aircraft identical?

No. Builders can select different engines, propellers, avionics, interiors, and other equipment. Each completed aircraft must be evaluated according to its own documentation and operating limitations.

### Can it carry an entire family?

No. The RV-6 has only two seats.

### Can it perform aerobatics?

Properly configured RV-6 aircraft can perform sport aerobatics within their approved weight and operating limitations. The pilot must also have appropriate training.

## Vocabulary

**Aerobatics:** Maneuvers involving unusual aircraft attitudes, such as rolls or loops.

**Avionics:** Electronic equipment used for communication, navigation, flight information, and aircraft monitoring.

**Constant-speed propeller:** A propeller that changes its blade angle to maintain a selected rotational speed.

**Experimental Amateur-Built:** An FAA airworthiness category for aircraft in which amateurs complete the majority of the fabrication and assembly work.

**Fixed-pitch propeller:** A propeller whose blade angle cannot be changed during flight.

**Fuselage:** The main body of an aircraft.

**Kit aircraft:** An aircraft supplied as components that a builder assembles into a complete airplane.

**Low-wing aircraft:** An airplane with its wings attached near the bottom of the fuselage.

**Stall:** A loss of lift caused when a wing exceeds its critical angle of attack.

**Tailwheel:** A small wheel beneath the aircraft’s tail, used with two main landing wheels.

**Tricycle landing gear:** Landing gear consisting of two main wheels and a nose wheel.
